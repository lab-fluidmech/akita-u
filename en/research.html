Research

The common thread in our work is order that emerges from interactions mediated by fluid flow: the arrangement of seeds falling as a group, the way particles gather at particular positions in a flow, and the vortex patterns that appear as a flow becomes turbulent can all be viewed in this light. We combine experiments (high-speed imaging, automatic tracking measurements), computational fluid dynamics (CFD) and theoretical analysis.

1. Autorotation and collective flight of wind-dispersed seeds

A maple seed (samara) autorotates on its single wing and descends slowly. We are testing the hypothesis that seeds released together from a parent tree fly as a group through hydrodynamic interactions, and that this widens their dispersal range.

  • Measuring descent motion (attitude, speed, angular velocity) with an automatic tracking camera
  • How seed shape and mass distribution affect flight
  • Theory and CFD analysis of the dynamic stability of autorotation
  • Experiments releasing several seeds at once, and analysis of the ordered structure of the group

This is an interdisciplinary project with an ecologist, Dr Kenji Narita (Faculty of Education and Human Studies, Akita University). We also have in view applications to rotary wings of small drones.
JSPS KAKENHI Grant-in-Aid for Scientific Research (C), 23K03334

2. Inertial focusing of particles in duct flows

Particles suspended in liquid flowing through a narrow duct or channel migrate to specific positions in the cross-section, a phenomenon known as inertial focusing. It has potential applications in microfluidic devices for separating blood cells or sorting fine particles.

  • Numerical analysis of the lift force on particles and their focusing positions in rectangular duct and channel flows
  • Transitions in focusing patterns with particle size

Joint research with Professor Masako Sugihara-Seki's group at Kansai University.

3. Flow stability, transition and pattern formation

An orderly (laminar) flow develops vortices as conditions change and eventually becomes turbulent. Along the way, a variety of vortex patterns appear and compete. We investigate these processes using bifurcation theory and high-accuracy numerical computation.

  • Transition and pattern competition in Taylor–Couette flow (flow between two rotating concentric cylinders)
  • Dynamics of inflow and outflow boundaries in rotating plane Couette flow
  • Transition in laterally heated shear flows in ducts and in stratified shear flows

International collaboration with Aston University, UK.

4. Aircraft wings and applied aerodynamics

Sucking a small amount of air through the wing surface keeps the boundary layer laminar and greatly reduces skin-friction drag (hybrid laminar flow control, HLFC). We are assessing the feasibility of a wing device that combines HLFC with anti-icing, reusing the blower that provides suction in cruise to supply anti-icing during climb and descent. We are also developing actuators that fit inside thin wings, in collaboration with Akita Industrial Technology Center.

  • System viability assessment of HLFC
  • Characteristics of flow drawn into perforated plates
  • Aerodynamic characteristics of cooling pads

JAXA Aviation Innovation Challenge 2026, feasibility study
